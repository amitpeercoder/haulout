# 010 — Wants and means

**Date:** 2026-11-08. **Decided by:** Amit (product). **Status:** adopted with proposed defaults; the open questions below are for Amit to overrule.

## Question

After 001–009 the sim has consequences but no desire. Money never comes in, debt costs nothing, the only goal is one party, and the reward for caring for the boat is to sell her. What makes a player want to fix things, and where does the money come from? (Amit, 2026-11-08: "what's the incentive? ... IRL it's work ... maybe it's a game of 'just one more turn', but how do we make it such?")

## Decisions

### 1. The week is the turn
Day 0 is a Monday; weekdays are work, weekends are boat days. Time aboard is the scarce thing, as it is for a real owner:
- **A weekday evening** is free: up to three hours aboard after work (placeholder), enough for a look, a small job, a charge left running. More than that on a weekday costs a **vacation day**.
- **A vacation budget** (placeholder 25 days a year) pays for weekdays aboard and for every weekday at sea. When it is spent, a weekday aboard is **unpaid leave**: a day's pay is docked.
- `WaitDays` stays for the hard; afloat the natural verbs are **next weekend** and **wait a week**. The week passing is the turn: payday, a part arriving, a window opening, a slot coming nearer.

### 2. Salary and costs
- **Pay** lands on the first of each month (placeholder: 6 000 NOK a month into the boat account, what is left of a salary after living costs). It is not shown as a salary; the status says "Payday: 6 000 NOK." The figure is the single economic knob of the game.
- **Standing costs** come due on the calendar and are deducted whether or not the player is there: berth and mooring (placeholder 12 000 NOK a year, billed quarterly), insurance (placeholder 8 000 NOK a year in spring; without it there is no payout), the yard's lift and storage as in 008.
- **Debt** is an overdraft with a limit (placeholder 20 000 NOK) and monthly interest (placeholder 1.5 %). Purchases beyond the limit are refused; bills that the player did not choose (a tow, a yard storage invoice) can breach it, and then nothing more can be bought until payday brings it back under. No loan sharks, no game over.
- **Parts take time**: anything not in the chandlery's stock is **ordered** and arrives in a seeded number of days (placeholder 3–10). Replace on a part not aboard orders it; the arrival is a notice. This makes "I'll just replace everything" cost weekends, not only money.

### 3. A calendar of wants
Events in data, each with a window, a place, a requirement and a story. Missed, they are gone for the year and the logbook says so. Placeholders:
- **The regatta party, Hestholmen**, Saturday of day 150 (the 007 goal becomes a dated event). Requires: be there.
- **Midsummer at Storholmen**, day 172–173: an overnight 26 nm away. Requires: fuel range or a sailing wind, nav lights for the late return.
- **A friend's week aboard**, a week in July the player picks: the friend comes only if the heads works, the stove works and the bunks are dry (no awash hours in the last month). The week spends vacation days; the friend pays for the fuel.
- **The club cruise to Kvitnes**, a weekend in August: an anchorage with no shop; requires anchor gear in order and a working bilge pump.
- **The autumn lift** as in 008.

### 4. Destinations have requirements
`PlanTrip` gains checks beyond readiness: **fuel** (the tank finally burns fuel: placeholder 2.5 L/h motoring at 5 kn, a 60 L tank; the plan says how many hours of motoring are aboard), **daylight** (hours of daylight from the series by month; a passage that ends after dark needs working navigation lights), **fog** (the series gains fog days by month; the harbour does not let you out in fog without a chartplotter or radar, and at sea fog slows you to 2 kn without them), and the **vacation days** the passage will cost. A blocker is shown in plain words; the player may still go where the rule is a risk, not a wall.

### 5. Upgrades are components
A short list of things the boat does not have, bought and fitted like any part (DIY hours or a yard day), then simulated like any component: condition, faults later, value at sale. Placeholders for the first round: **diesel heater** (spring and autumn comfort; the friend's week in a cold July), **marine heads** (the friend's requirement), **stove** (same), **chartplotter** (fog, night), **radar** (fog without the plotter's limits; expensive), **navigation lights** (exist, may be faulty), **new bunk cushions** (dry bunks), **cockpit varnish** (value only). Each adds to the broker's letter. Upgrades carry their own faults in later rounds (the plotter's power feed, the heater's glow pin). They never add to a stat; they open a want.

### 6. The yardstick is the logbook, not a score
At the end of a season (the autumn lift) the logbook writes a **season page**: miles sailed, nights aboard, places reached, events made and missed, hours worked, money spent, what she is worth against what she cost. No grade. **Reputation** at the yard and the harbour is a small number the player sees in words ("the yard knows you pay on time"), built from tows, parted lines, late bills and missed slots, and it moves the tow price and the slot the yard offers. Steam achievements are a later adapter concern; candidates are listed in the implementation notes, not built.

### 7. Year two is the loop
`Keep` becomes the default pull: the calendar repeats with the boat as she is, upgrades and all, the budget renews, and the season page from year one sits in the logbook. The sale stays for the player who wants to trade up; it is no longer the only ending.

## Guardrails

- No scripted economy: every bill, arrival and event is a date or a rate in data with a seeded spread where it is uncertain.
- The player never sees a number they could not know aboard a real boat (rule 5): the account balance, the pay, the bills, the vacation days left, the forecast.
- Nothing here changes the fault model; wants pull on it.
- Chill: debt caps, nobody is fired. The pain is a missed midsummer, not a game over.

### Step 1 — the week and the money

**The weekday rule.** `Tick` already counts `_workMinutesToday` on aboard steps. Add, per day:
- `_chargedDay` (the last day charged) and the charge itself in `ChargeDay(Turn t, reason)`: on a **weekday**, the moment (inside an aboard step) the day's work minutes pass `eveningMinutes`, or the first step of a weekday at sea (aboard or not; a passage is always the player's day), the day is charged once: a vacation day if any are left this year, else **unpaid leave** (a bill of `unpaidLeaveNok`, ledger kind `unpaid`). The narrative gets one line at that moment: "That's more than an evening's work: today, Tuesday, becomes a vacation day (24 left this year)." / "A weekday at sea: today, Thursday, is a vacation day (23 left this year)." / "No vacation days left: today, Wednesday, is unpaid leave. 1500 NOK comes off your pay." and a logbook line ("Vacation day: Tuesday, day 79." / "Unpaid leave: Wednesday, day 80.", the latter with its cost). Weekends and away time (waits, the yard's days, the survey) never charge. The narrative line goes to the `Turn` being executed; a step without a turn in hand (none: every Tick has one) is not a case.
- The vacation year is the calendar year: at 00:00 on day of year 0 the days left go back to `vacationDaysPerYear`. Year 1 starts with the full budget (decided: no pro rata).
- Counters for the season page and score: `VacationDaysUsed` and `UnpaidDays` this year.

**Money: the ledger.** Replace the bare `_money`/`_incomeNok` arithmetic with a ledger, keeping their meaning:
- `LedgerEntry(int Day, string Kind, string Text, int Nok)`; Kind one of `income` (pay, the sale), `spending` (whatever the player chose: Accept's `costNok`), `bill` (standing costs, the tow, the parted mooring's bill, the lift and storage at `haul-out` and the autumn emergency lift's storage), `interest`, `unpaid`. Nok is signed: income positive, everything else negative.
- One door: `Spend(int nok, string kind, string text)` and `Earn(int nok, string text)`. `Accept` books its `costNok` through `Spend` (kind `bill` for the actions `call-tow` and `haul-out`, else `spending`); the tow, the parted mooring and the sale go through the same door. `_money` stays "everything that went out" (`MoneySpentNok` in status and the closing card keep their meaning: spending + bills + interest + unpaid leave); `_incomeNok` becomes pay + the sale. `Balance = start − out + income` exactly as today. `StatusView.Balance` means what it meant.
- **Payday** at 00:00 on the first day of each month (in `Daily(day)` when `(day + 1) % 30 == 0`, booked on day `day + 1`): first **interest** (if the balance is below zero: `ceil(−Balance · interestPerMonth)`, kind `interest`, text "Overdraft interest for the month."), then the **pay** (kind `income`, text "Payday."). Then the **bills** due that day (in data order). Each is a logbook line with its cost (pay as a line without cost but "Payday: 6000 NOK."), and an alert: `payday` "Payday: 6000 NOK." (the note's status wording), `bill-<id>` "The berth invoice came: 3000 NOK for the quarter." (text from data + nok), `interest` "The bank took 210 NOK of overdraft interest." None of these stops any wait (they are not in `NeedsHands` or `MooringStops`).
- **The overdraft.** `MoneyRefusal(cost)` refuses when `Balance − cost < −overdraftLimitNok`: "That's N NOK, and you have M NOK left before the bank's limit (20000 NOK overdrawn)." when the balance is still above the limit; "You're N NOK overdrawn, past the bank's limit of 20000: nothing more until payday brings you back under." when it is already past. With the default (no calendar) the limit is 0 and today's two texts stay exactly as they are ("That's N NOK and you have M NOK left." / "…you owe N NOK already."). Bills the player did not choose (tow, storage, parted mooring, standing bills, interest, unpaid leave) are never refused and may breach the limit.
- `_lastBillDay` etc. not needed; the "late bill" count for reputation (step 4) is recorded now: `_billsPastLimit` += 1 whenever a `bill` leaves the balance past the limit.

**Parts take time** (`Game.Orders.cs`).
- `Order(string Item)` (JSON `order`): a phone call to the yard chandlery from anywhere not at sea (`phoneMinutes`, the player's own time but never a vacation day by itself). Resolves a chandlery item by name (as `Buy`) or, failing that, a **seen component whose spec is not stocked** ("starter motor"). Pays the price at once (`spending`), the lead time is `leadDays` lo..hi by `SeasonRoll(9, orderNumber, Day)` (whole days, inclusive), delivered at 08:00 on `Day + lead` to `deliverTo`. Refused: at sea ("You're at sea: ring the yard from port."), unknown name, already on order ("The starter motor is on order already: due on day N."), money.
- `Buy` of an item with `Stocked: false` at the home port (the yard chandlery) is the same order ("The yard chandlery doesn't keep a diesel heater on the shelf: ordered, due on day N."). At another port the shop's stock rule is unchanged, and the refusal adds "…You can order it from the yard (order <item>)." for an item the yard sells.
- `Replace` of a component with `Stocked: false`, no spare aboard, in port: **refused with the order offered**, not an automatic order (decided: an order costs money, and a refusal that spends is not a refusal): "The yard chandlery doesn't keep a starter motor on the shelf. Order one (order starter motor): about 3 to 10 days." While one is on order: "The starter motor is on order: due on day N." While it is waiting at the yard and she is elsewhere: "The new starter motor is waiting for you at the yard." At sea the spare rule is unchanged.
- Delivery: in `Daily`, for each order due the next morning, at 00:00 it becomes `Arrived` (delivered at 08:00 is narration only). If she is at `deliverTo` (afloat in port or on the hard), it goes aboard into the spares (`_inventory` for a chandlery item; `_partsAboard[componentId]` for a component part, shown in the inventory as "starter motor (new, in its box)"), alert `delivery` "The yard chandlery rang: your starter motor has come in. It's aboard." Else it waits at the yard, alert `delivery` "…It's waiting for you at the yard.", and goes aboard (narrative line "At the yard: the starter motor that came in is put aboard.") on the next arrival at, or command at, `deliverTo`. `SpareFor(componentId)` also finds `_partsAboard`; `Replace` consumes it (no money: it was paid for). A delivery is a logbook line; not a phone call stop.
- **The waits stop for the calendar.** `StopAtSlot` becomes `StopAtCalendar(t, days)`: a wait that would pass the morning of the booked slot, of a due delivery, or (step 2) of the first day of an open event window, stops on that morning at the same time of day, with "You come back on day N: <the starter motor is due at the yard | your haulout slot | Midsummer at Storholmen starts today>." The first of them wins. A wait that starts on that day is not stopped by it (as 008).

**Next weekend and wait a week.**
- `NextWeekend()` (JSON `next-weekend`): not at sea; waits until **08:00 on the next Saturday** (from Saturday or Sunday: the Saturday after; from a weekday: the coming one). `WaitWeek()` (JSON `wait-week`): until 08:00 seven days on (`Day + 7`). Both pass the time away (no vacation days), shut the engine down, go through `StopAtCalendar`, and run exactly like `WaitDays` otherwise: **afloat at a port** they are the harbourmaster's day-by-day wait (`DoWaitMoored`, generalised to take minutes: the first step is the part-day to midnight, then whole days, stopping on `MooringStops` as today); on the hard a single away tick. Refused at sea "You're at sea: continue, heave to or anchor." The narrative's first line: "You go back to work. Saturday, day 82: you're back aboard at eight." (or the cut-short wording of the harbourmaster/`StopAtCalendar`), then the weather summary as `WaitDays`, then a **week line** of what came in: "While you were away: payday 6000 NOK; the berth invoice 3000 NOK; the starter motor came in at the yard." (from the ledger and deliveries of the wait; omitted when nothing happened). Log line "Waited until Saturday, day 82." / "Waited a week."
- `WaitDays` is unchanged in use (the hard, the winter, any number of days) and also gets the week line.

### Step 2 — fuel, daylight, fog, and the calendar of wants

**Weather additions** (`weather.json`, each month gains two keys, flagged UNVERIFIED in its `placeholders` text: western-Norway-ish guesses around 60° N):

| month | J | F | M | A | M | J | J | A | S | O | N | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `daylightHours` | 6.5 | 9 | 11.5 | 14.5 | 17 | 19 | 18.5 | 15.5 | 12.5 | 10 | 7.5 | 6 |
| `fogChance` | 0.03 | 0.03 | 0.05 | 0.08 | 0.10 | 0.08 | 0.06 | 0.05 | 0.05 | 0.04 | 0.04 | 0.03 |

plus top-level `fogAllDayFraction` 0.25 and `fogLiftsHour` [9, 13]. `MonthClimate` gains `double DaylightHours = 12, double FogChance = 0` (optional, so a climate without them still loads).
- `WeatherDay` gains trailing `double DaylightHours = 24, int FogUntilHour = 0` (defaults: never dark, no fog, so every hand-made series in the tests is unchanged). `Generate` sets `DaylightHours` by the same month-centre blend as the other curves (no randomness) and draws fog from its **own** stream (`new SeededRandom(seed * 7349 + 101)`, two draws per day, every day, whatever the outcome): fog with `fogChance`; then all day (`FogUntilHour` 24) with `fogAllDayFraction`, else lifting at a whole hour in `fogLiftsHour`. The golden hash test of days 0 to 720 must still pass untouched.
- Dark: an hour of day `h` (0–24, fractional) is dark when `|h − solarNoonHour| > DaylightHours / 2`. `WeatherWords`: `IsFoggy(day, hour)`, `Describe` adds "Fog over the water." while `hour < FogUntilHour` (the view knows the hour of `Today`), and the forecast day gains `bool Fog` (true when the day has any fog; the forecast's noise does not touch fog: it is a morning you see coming), `ForecastDay` trailing `bool Fog = false`, text "morning fog" / "fog all day".

**Fuel** (`fuel` block). The tank is `litres: 60, fuelLitres: 27` (same fraction as 18 of 40, so the condensation weighting of 008 is unchanged; the fill-tank cost is now 33 L at 22).
- Burn in `SeaBefore`/`SystemsStep`: while the engine runs, `fuelLitres −= (motoring under way ? motorLitresPerHour : idleLitresPerHour) · h`, floored at 0. Under way motoring is `SeaMode.Motoring`; everything else that runs the engine (in port, hove-to with it running, on the hard on the yard hose, charging) is idle.
- Empty: `EngineInputsBuilder` treats `fuelLitres < 0.5` as a fully restricted fuel supply, so the engine model's own words apply (it cranks and does not fire; under way it fires and dies). Under way the existing `fuel` alert fires, with a gauge-reading variant when the tank is the cause: kind `fuel-empty`, "The engine coughs and dies. The fuel gauge needle lies on empty." `CheckFilterBowl` with an empty tank: "The bowl is half air. Nothing is coming through from the tank." `Inspect fuel tank` adds the level in words.
- The gauge: `StatusView` gains `string? Fuel = null` ("Fuel: about 25 litres (a little under half a tank).", litres rounded to `gaugeStepLitres`). The player reads a gauge; the exact litres are not shown anywhere.
- `FillTank` is unchanged in rule (ports with `fuel`), priced as today; during the friend's week it is free (the friend pays; ledger text "Diesel, paid by your friend." at 0, logged).

**Night and fog at sea** (`night` block).
- Nav lights "working" = the `nav-lights` component has no present fault. ### Decided where the note was silent (lead, before the work)

- The clock does not move to the evening: a weekday "evening" is the first `eveningMinutes` of work that day whatever the game hour shows. The game's hours are bookkeeping; the day is the unit of a real owner's time.
- A vacation day is charged at the moment it is incurred (the minute work passes the evening, the first step of a weekday at sea), not at midnight, so the player is told while choosing, and a later job the same day costs nothing more.
- Unpaid leave docks 1500 NOK from the boat account: the boat account bears it because the living costs are fixed. Placeholder; the note says "a day's pay".
- The first berth bill is day 90 and the first insurance day 450: the season's first quarter and the year's insurance were paid with the boat. With the shipped numbers the first year pays 9 × 6000 = 54000, bills 9000 berth + 16000 lift and storage: from the starting 25000 that leaves about 54000 for parts, upgrades, fuel and tows, before any overdraft. (The report says whether that is too loose.)
- Ordering is a separate command; `Replace` of an unstocked part refuses and names the order (a refusal never spends). `Buy` at the yard of an unstocked item is the order, since buying is already spending.
- Deliveries go to the yard; elsewhere they wait for her there.
- Waits stop on the morning of a delivery, an event's first day and the booked slot (all three are dates the player was told), at the same time of day; `NextWeekend` and `WaitWeek` end at 08:00.
- Night: fines, not walls; the truth of the lights decides, the plan only states what the player knows. Fog: a wall in harbour (the note's rule), a speed cap at sea, lifting at a seeded hour.
- Event requirements are judged against the truth at the event, and a failure discovers the fault behind it (the event is a symptom). Views show only what the player knows.
