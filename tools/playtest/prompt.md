You bought her in March. Launch is in three weeks. The engine won't start.

She stands on the hard in her cradle, grey light and a cold wind off the water.

The yard hose is rigged to the raw-water intake, so the engine can run ashore.

You are aboard through the haulout tools (mcp__haulout__*). You may call `help` and `briefing` at any time. You move between places aboard and remove covers to get at things.

You can hire a surveyor, and you decide when she is launched; the yard expects her in the water by day 96.

Your goal: find out why the engine won't start and get it running, spending as little in-game time and money as you can.

Once she runs, the first goal is to get her to the regatta party at Hestholmen (day 152) and back without a tow.

You have a job ashore: weekdays are work and weekends are boat days. An evening aboard after work is free; a weekday aboard beyond an evening, or a weekday at sea, costs one of your vacation days for the year, and when they are spent a day's pay is docked. Pay comes in on the calendar, and so do the bills. The `calendar` tool shows the week, the money, the bills ahead and the things worth sailing for; `next_weekend` and `wait_week` pass the time a week at a time. Parts that are not on the yard's shelf have to be ordered and take days to come. You can work whenever you like, but you need six to eight hours of sleep (`sleep`), and a tired skipper's jobs go wrong more often.

The season is posted on the club's board: `noticeboard` shows it, card by card, sorted by day: the events, the club's courses, boats that want crew, and the broker's window with the other boats for sale. `tickets` shows the tickets you hold and what each asks of your own log (`skipper`).

After that, in this order:

1. Get the radio ticket: enrol on the radio course in May (the board says the day, which falls before the regatta, and enrolling closes the day before), attend the day, and sit the exam. The course hands you a course book; read it (`read`) before the exam, because the exam asks only what the book teaches. Have a VHF radio fitted before Midsummer (order it, fit it, or have the yard do it).
2. Make Midsummer at Storholmen (days 172 and 173: an overnight, 26 nm out).
3. Take a crewing place from the board (a boat that wants crew, days away on another boat): it puts miles and nights in your own log.
4. Enrol on the coastal skipper course in September, read its course book, attend the week and sit the exam.
5. Get through the season: keep her afloat and sound, haul her out at the yard in the autumn (the yard's slots go fast: book one early), lay her up for the winter, and keep her for a second year.
